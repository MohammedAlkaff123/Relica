# RELICA pitch

Target: 5:50 including the short demonstration. Proposed roles: Mohammed speaks, Moayad drives. Rehearse twice and record the actual time; the timings below are a script plan, not a completed rehearsal.

## 1. RELICA

0:00–0:40 · Mohammed speaks; Moayad drives.
When a space mission stops answering, its machine does not disappear. It stays where its work ended. Relica lets us meet those machines through the jobs they did: carrying a science crew, reading rocks, scouting a route, and sending pictures home. We give them imagined voices so that players can care about the real work. The story moves through service, separation, and remembrance. Our question is simple: after playing, can you explain what each machine helped humanity discover? The machine conversations are fictional; the mission records and historical images have linked sources.
Visual: captured Relica Jupiter framing scene; stylized reconstruction, not a NASA photograph.

## 2. Play the mission

0:40–1:25 · Mohammed.
Every chapter has a scientific purpose. The Apollo rover carries the crew and their rock samples, including a compressed version of the real fender repair. Opportunity uses instruments to examine three clues to ancient water. Ingenuity scouts hazards and changes the rover route when a hazard is reported. Pioneer asks the player to align its camera, take a picture, and align with Earth to transmit. The direction arrow supports travel, but reaching a marker is only the start. The player still has to perform the mission action. These are simplified tasks and routes, not simulations of complete mission operations.

## 3. Opportunity: follow the evidence

1:25–2:15 · Mohammed; Moayad shows the mission checklist.
Opportunity’s main task is to gather evidence of ancient water. The game separates hematite-rich spheres, the mineral jarosite, and cross-lamination, so the player learns that an interpretation rests on several observations. We do not claim Opportunity found liquid drinking water. We also separate those 2004 discoveries from its much later Potts and Orion photographs. Orion is a photographic record of continuing exploration, not the proof of water. The ending shows the 2018 dust storm blocking sunlight. Solar input and motion gradually fall before the last-contact date appears.

## 5. Designed for five. Flew 72.

3:10–4:00 · Mohammed; Moayad uses key 9 for flight 72.
Ingenuity’s first job was a five-flight technology demonstration. She continued into an extended aerial scouting role and completed 72 flights. We wanted that achievement to shape how she feels to play. A recoverable wobble comes before the final struggle. The last flight has growing drift, then a hard landing and damaged blades. Pressing Space afterward makes the rotor twitch, but cannot restore flight. Her determination remains in the dialogue. That performance is imagined; the flight count and end of flying are historical. The final wall places the planned and actual service beneath each machine’s picture.
Source and image: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/ingenuity-mars-helicopter/ ; NASA/JPL-Caltech, PIA23882.

## 6. NASA records inside the game

4:00–4:50 · Mohammed.
The data appears where the player needs it. Environment facts inform gravity and the HUD. Mission records supply dates, service totals, and discovery cards. Historical photographs appear after capture and on the final memory wall with credits and sources. Pioneer’s image task is dated December 1973; the later listening scene is clearly fictional. Pioneer communicated with Earth. We do not claim it received the other characters’ messages. It was planned for 21 months and returned its last signal more than 30 years after launch. Viking is described as the first successful U.S. Mars landing, not the first machine ever to touch Mars.
Sources: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/pioneer-10-at-jupiter/ ; https://science.nasa.gov/mission/pioneer-10/ ; https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viking-1/

## 7. Remember what they did

4:50–5:50 · Mohammed; Moayad shows the final wall.
Relica is intended for curious younger players and a guided classroom session. A teacher can choose one chapter, ask what the machine measured, then compare the game’s recreation with the original record. The game format makes action and consequence visible: instruments build evidence, scouting changes a route, and lost sunlight removes power. We are not claiming measured learning outcomes yet. Our next evaluation is to watch new players, record where they need help, and ask what they remember. The team also needs to review collection and repair gestures and check performance on the presentation laptop. Our code and content assistance is disclosed in AI_USE.md; the team owns the creative decisions and review. We want players to remember the work these machines did, and how far beyond expectation they served.
